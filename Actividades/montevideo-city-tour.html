Itinerary

Approximate duration: 4 hours. Duration is approximate and is not a fixed timetable. Stops are viewed from the tour; interior visits and tickets are not included unless stated.

  • Plaza IndependenciaMontevideo’s most emblematic square, surrounded by historic buildings and presided over by the imposing Artigas monument. From here you reach Ciudad Vieja and Avenida 18 de Julio, the heart of the capital.
  • Catedral de San Felipe y SantiagoMontevideo’s main Catholic church, on Plaza Constitución. Its interior preserves valuable religious works and it is a symbol of the city’s colonial heritage.
  • Plaza ConstituciónAlso known as Plaza Matriz, it is the oldest square in Montevideo. Surrounded by cafés, historic buildings, and craft fairs, it invites you to enjoy the colonial atmosphere of Ciudad Vieja.
  • Teatro SolísOpened in 1856, it is one of Latin America’s most prestigious theaters. Its architecture and cultural program make it an essential stop.
  • Playa PocitosIn one of Montevideo’s most modern neighborhoods, this beach is ideal for walking the rambla and enjoying the urban landscape on the Río de la Plata.
  • Letras de MontevideoThe famous “Montevideo” sign in front of Playa Pocitos is one of the city’s most recognized photo spots.
  • Estadio CentenarioA historic monument of world football and venue of the first World Cup in 1930. Visiting it is a way to discover part of Uruguay’s sporting identity.
  • Monumento a la CarretaIn Parque Batlle, it represents rural life in 19th-century Uruguay. Its scale and detail make it one of the city’s most iconic sculptures.
  • Palacio LegislativoSeat of the Uruguayan Parliament, inaugurated in 1925. It stands out for its majestic marble architecture.
  • Mercado turísticoIn the heart of Ciudad Vieja, this space brings together crafts, souvenirs, and typical Uruguayan products.
